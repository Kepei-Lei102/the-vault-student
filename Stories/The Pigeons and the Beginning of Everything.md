# The Pigeons and the Beginning of Everything 鸽子与宇宙的回声

> *Before the universe became the explanation, the pigeons had to stop being one.*

## Cast of Characters

- **Arno Penzias** — a refugee child from Munich who became a radio astronomer and built an unusually careful reference source.
- **Robert Wilson** — a Houston boy who repaired radios; later, the partner who would not stop checking a troublesome antenna.
- **Robert Dicke, Jim Peebles, Peter Roll and David Wilkinson** — the Princeton group with a cosmological question and an experiment of their own.
- **George Gamow, Ralph Alpher and Robert Herman** — earlier explorers of a hot early universe; Alpher and Herman had already estimated its surviving radiation.
- **Two pigeons** — tenants with no apparent interest in cosmology.

## 中文锚点

## Act I — The machine is too warm

At Bell Laboratories in Holmdel, New Jersey, two young radio astronomers had a problem with the sky.

Their instrument registered more microwave power than they could account for. They were measuring at 4080 megacycles per second—today we write **4.08 GHz**, corresponding to a wavelength of about **7.35 cm**. Their published result would be an unexplained excess equivalent to **3.5 ± 1.0 K**. [Penzias and Wilson, 1965](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1965ApJ...142..419P)

That does **not** mean somebody put a thermometer against the antenna and found it almost at absolute zero. A radio astronomer's *noise temperature* expresses the strength of a fluctuating electrical signal on a temperature scale. It lets contributions from different sources be compared in one set of units.

Nor did “noise” establish that the signal was worthless. It named the nuisance they were trying to understand.

If you are building an instrument, an unexplained offset is usually a reason to check your instrument. Announcing a new universe is a fairly extravagant first repair strategy.

They started with the instrument.

## Act II — Two people who knew their way around a receiver

Wilson's childhood in Houston had included repairing radios and, later, television sets. Electronics came through his engineer father; repair work brought pleasure and spending money. Physics at Rice and radio astronomy at Caltech turned that practical interest into a research career. Bell Labs recruited him in 1963. [AIP's biographical account](https://history.aip.org/exhibits/cosmology/tools/tools-penzias-wilson.htm)

Keep the radio-repairing boy in view. The adult discovery would depend on knowing how many ordinary things can make a receiver misbehave.

Penzias had come to the United States by a much less secure route. In 1939, aged six, he and his younger brother escaped Nazi Germany on a Kindertransport to England; their parents subsequently joined them, and the family reached America. Decades later, explaining why the receiver ended up in Munich's Deutsches Museum, he connected the instrument's destination to the city he had been forced to leave. His scientific life and his childhood had not become separate stories. [Markus Pössel's conversation with Penzias, Lindau Nobel](https://www.lindau-nobel.org/de/arno-penzias-die-kosmische-hintergrundstrahlung-und-das-deutsche-museum/)

By 1963, the research phases of the Echo and Telstar satellite-communications projects were ending, making Bell Labs' 20-foot horn-reflector antenna available for astronomy. Penzias and Wilson planned accurate measurements of radio sources, including the Milky Way. Communications engineering had supplied them with exceptionally good equipment. [Penzias's 1981 account](https://garfield.library.upenn.edu/classics1981/A1981LL57400001.pdf)

Usually, an astronomer can compare a source with a neighbouring patch of relatively empty sky. But what if the faint emission you want to measure is spread across the sky? You need a reference whose own signal is known. Penzias built a reference termination cooled with liquid helium. Its accuracy helped make the excess impossible to dismiss as an imprecise zero. [Penzias, 1981](https://garfield.library.upenn.edu/classics1981/A1981LL57400001.pdf)

Good equipment had made their problem harder to ignore.

## Act III — The tenants

A pair of pigeons had found a comfortable place in the narrow end of the antenna.

A radio telescope is meant to collect a very small signal from a very large distance. A bird is a warm, locally available complication. Its deposits are also material inside the receiving structure: something that could alter losses and contribute unwanted emission.

Wilson later described the coating as:

> “a white material familiar to all city dwellers.”

They removed the pigeons and cleaned the mess. The antenna temperature fell only slightly. Further cleaning, checks of the throat and attention to the metal seams still did not account for the excess. [Wilson's Nobel lecture, p.475](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/wilson-lecture-1.pdf)

*Imagined reconstruction in a 1960s magazine-comic style. The antenna geometry, poses and meter are simplified; these are not documentary images or historical readings. The documented joke is that cleaning the apparatus did not make the unexplained signal go away.*

There is an easy version of this scene in which the scientists look silly for blaming birds.

Try working on a sensitive receiver that contains bird droppings. Cleaning it is an excellent idea.

The useful question comes **after** the cleaning: *how much did the result change?* A plausible explanation must pay off in the measurement. Once the improvement had been measured, it could not simply be enlarged in imagination to cover the rest.

## Act IV — The remainder survives

The 1965 paper gives a compact account of the subtraction:

| Contribution | Equivalent antenna temperature |
|---|---:|
| Measured total looking upward | 6.7 K |
| Atmospheric contribution | 2.3 K |
| Antenna losses and response to radiation behind it | 0.9 K |
| Remaining excess | 3.5 K |

The residual is $6.7-2.3-0.9=3.5\ \mathrm K$. The authors also reported an uncertainty: **±1.0 K**. That qualification belongs to the result. The excess showed no detectable preferred direction or polarization and no seasonal change within their observations from July 1964 to April 1965. [Penzias and Wilson, 1965, pp.419–420](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1965ApJ...142..419P)

The important achievement was not subtraction. It was earning the numbers that could legitimately be subtracted.

A repeatable reading alone would not have settled the matter. A faulty zero can repeat beautifully. They had to change conditions, check contributions and see which explanations survived. [[Calibration of Instruments]] and [[Accuracy vs Precision]] are practical here: a stable instrument can be stably misleading.

Their report left a small number sitting in public, with its uncertainty attached, asking for an explanation.

## Act V — A different question, a short distance away

At Princeton, Dicke's group was approaching the sky from the other direction: a hot, dense early universe should leave a bath of radiation as it expands and cools. Roll and Wilkinson were constructing a radiometer to look for it near a wavelength of 3 cm. Their theoretical paper would discuss several possible cosmic histories, including an oscillating universe; it was not a statement of today's fully developed cosmological model. [Dicke, Peebles, Roll and Wilkinson, 1965](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1965ApJ...142..414D)

The two groups had complementary difficulties. One had a measurement without a satisfactory interpretation. The other had a reason to search, and an instrument not yet producing its result.

Penzias later recalled that a colleague drew his attention to Peebles's work. Contact with Dicke connected the problems. The groups arranged adjacent papers: the Princeton team discussed the cosmological interpretation, and Penzias and Wilson reported the observation. [Penzias, 1981](https://garfield.library.upenn.edu/classics1981/A1981LL57400001.pdf)

Dicke is remembered as responding:

> “Boys, we've been scooped!”

That is the later-recounted line, not a transcript from a recording. It captures a real disappointment: someone else had reached the signal first. [AIP's history of the discovery](https://history.aip.org/exhibits/cosmology/tools/tools-new.htm)

The response that mattered next was cooperation. A disappointing race result could still become a productive exchange between people who understood different parts of the problem.

## Act VI — The title that refused to shout

Their observational paper was called *A Measurement of Excess Antenna Temperature at 4080 Mc/s*.

You could file that title beside maintenance documentation and never suspect what was inside. Its restrained language distinguished what they had measured from the explanation offered in the neighbouring paper. [Original paper](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1965ApJ...142..419P)

There is a kindness to future readers in that separation. A measurement with its method and uncertainties remains useful even if a proposed interpretation needs revision.

And Princeton was not the beginning of the idea. In 1948, Gamow, Alpher and Herman had developed arguments about a hot early universe; Alpher and Herman made an early estimate of its surviving radiation temperature. Peebles's later historical analysis follows the changing assumptions across that year's papers. The prediction did not emerge as one finished, universally understood argument and then wait neatly for a detector. [Peebles, *What happened in 1948*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.2146)

That history makes the discovery more interesting. Ideas can exist without reaching the people whose instruments could test them. Having a prediction somewhere in print is different from connecting it to an experiment.

## Act VII — A point becomes a spectrum

One measurement at one frequency cannot establish an entire blackbody spectrum.

The Princeton paper explicitly said more measurements were needed and described the wavelengths the teams intended to investigate. The first result created work for other instruments and other people. [Dicke and colleagues, 1965, p.416](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1965ApJ...142..414D)

Later measurements supplied the spectrum. COBE's FIRAS instrument found extraordinarily close agreement with a blackbody at about **2.725 K**. That is a later precision result; it should not be retroactively inserted into Penzias and Wilson's 1965 measurement. [NASA's FIRAS overview](https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/product/cobe/firas_overview.html)

The cosmic microwave background is ancient light that could travel freely once the early plasma became largely neutral, about **380,000 years** after the Big Bang. Cosmic expansion subsequently stretched its wavelengths. It is not sound travelling through space, and it is not a photograph of the instant the universe began. The “echo” is a metaphor. [[Hubble's Law and the Expanding Universe]] develops the physical explanation. [ESA's CMB account](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Planck/The_cosmic_microwave_background_and_inflation)

In 1978, Penzias and Wilson shared half of the Nobel Prize in Physics for the discovery; the other half went to Pyotr Kapitza for separate work in low-temperature physics. [Nobel award record](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1978/summary/)

The prize gives the story a convenient ending. The measurements did not end there.

## Cultural ripples — being willing to keep the awkward number

The famous version involves pigeons. The useful version also involves a reference source, a sequence of checks, two collaborating groups and predecessors whose work was not yet connected to the apparatus.

That is recognisable far outside astronomy. A microphone still hums after you replace the cable. A program still fails after the suspicious line has been rewritten. A kitchen scale returns the same implausible number five times. Repeating the conclusion “it must be the equipment” does not identify the fault. Each attempted fix must answer a question about what changed.

Nor does an unexplained remainder automatically become a discovery. Most faults remain faults. The discipline is to keep its status honest: *this much is explained; this much is still open*.

Imagine the interval between cleaning the antenna and hearing from Princeton. The equipment was cleaner. The explanation was still missing. The researchers had not yet earned the satisfying ending we already know.

They kept the number anyway.

## Try the evidence

Open page 420 of the [original report](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1965ApJ...142..419P). Find one measured contribution, one calculated contribution and one test that failed to produce the expected change. Then ask which of them you would challenge first if the unexplained remainder were yours.

The point is to practise following a claim back to the operation that produced it. The uncertainty belongs to that chain too.
