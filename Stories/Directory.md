## What this folder is for

`Mathematics/`, `Physics/`, `Meta/` are pedagogy-first — the math leads, the story is incidental. **Stories/** flips it: fun-first, rigor incidental. The same way [[Heptadecagon]] uses Gauss-at-19 because the story IS the pedagogical answer, Stories cards use story for its own sake — because the human drama of how mathematics actually got made is genuinely worth knowing, and a vault that's only proofs is missing half the spirit.

The vault's "fun is the spirit" north star, made structural.

## The dual-residency principle

For story-rich topics that already live in pedagogical cards (Newton-Leibniz priority dispute, Bernoulli-L'Hôpital naming injustice, the discovery of $e$, Gauss at 19, the invention of variance, etc.), we run **dual residency**:

- **The pedagogical card** keeps what's *load-bearing* for the math — usually a compressed historical version that motivates the math, plus a 1-2 line cross-reference + link.
- **The Stories card** carries the *fuller dramatic version* with material that wouldn't earn a place in the pedagogical card — primary-source quotes, contextual detail, ripples beyond the immediate math, named figures' biographical color.
- **They cross-link both ways.** A student reading the pedagogical card sees one paragraph and a link; a student following the link gets the full drama; both are legitimate doors into the same topic.

For born-in-Stories topics (the War of the Currents, Galois' duel, Ramanujan's letter, etc.) there's no pedagogical residue — the story stands alone in this folder.

34. **[[Stories/The Loudness War|The Loudness War]]** ✅ landed 2026-08-11 (Queue S35) — the sequel to [[Stories/One Take, Many Tracks]]: what producers did with the total control that story handed them. Seven acts. **The referee was a needle** — a lacquer groove physically punishes loudness (wider wiggles, less music per side, and past a point the stylus jumps out), so for thirty years there was an incentive to be loud and a governor made of metal. **The CD removed the governor** (1982): 0 dBFS is an absolute ceiling on the *peak* and says nothing whatever about the *average*, and the entire war lives in that gap — crest factor as the real casualty. **Why the loud one always wins the audition**: Fletcher–Munson's 1933 equal-loudness contours mean a louder playback sounds *fuller*, not merely bigger, so an unmatched A/B is a rigged comparison — which makes the war **a tragedy of the commons**, not a failure of taste; every individual choice is correct and the shared resource is consumed anyway. **The ratchet**: brickwall limiting, makeup gain, then outright clipping. **The Kemmler moment — *Death Magnetic* (2008)**, whose best-sounding release was a *plastic-guitar video game*: Activision had the stems before the crushing, players ripped the game audio and A/B'd it, a petition passed thirteen thousand signatures, and mastering engineer Ted Jensen publicly declined to own his own master (the mixes arrived brickwalled). **An apology in album form**: Rush's *Vapor Trails* (2002) not remastered but **remixed** from the multitracks eleven years later — Geddy Lee, *"We overcooked it."* **And the treaty was a measurement**: ITU-R BS.1770 (2006) turns a whole track into one LUFS number, EBU R 128 (2010) makes it policy, streaming adopts it — and the incentive **inverts**, because the platform turns your loud master *down*. Normalise a −7.8 LUFS remaster beside its −18.8 LUFS original and the dynamic one still peaks at −4.5 dBTP while the crushed one has nothing above −13.7: **nine decibels of headroom at identical loudness**, so the crushed master arrives sounding *smaller*. Honest edges: loud is not a synonym for bad (some genres want the wall), normalisation is defeatable, the DR number became its own fetish, engineers took blame they usually hadn't earned — and the ending is not noble, since nobody repented, it just stopped paying. Ripples: the **compression word-collision** (data vs dynamic-range — two unrelated injuries), and a tragedy of the commons that actually got *solved*, by an agreed measurement rather than by virtue. Dual-residency [[Sound Encoding]] (the 96 dB budget being spent); [[Logarithms]] carries why louder wins. Normalisation figure verified light + dark; comic is the Guitar Hero bedroom moment — two waveform strips taped to the wall, one alive, one a solid brick, captioned *THE GAME SOUNDED BETTER.* **Expanded same sitting:** a **mixing vs mastering** callout now opens Act V, because the act's punchline is meaningless without it — *mixing is about how the parts of a song relate to each other; mastering is about how the song relates to everything else* — with the historical note that mastering began as a **transfer** job at the lathe (the Act I cutting engineer, solving physical problems) and the modern blur that lets a limiter on the mix bus deliver a pre-crushed mix to the person whose name goes on the sleeve. And a new **Act VIII** tests the thesis twice more: **Dolby Atmos tightened the treaty** (object-based audio, but shipped with a *stricter* −18 LUFS / −1 dBTP spec the renderer enforces, plus space substituting for level as a way to make impact — and a catalogue that converted fast because Apple pays ~10% more royalty for spatial audio, the same incentive mechanism one floor up), while **short-form video reopened it** (phone speaker in a noisy room makes dynamics actively harmful; sub-second in-feed comparison restores Act III's rigged A/B; normalisation inconsistent and re-uploads stacked — visible in the writing as hooks moved to the top, intros deleted, official sped-up variants). Sharper moral than "the war ended": **the treaty holds exactly where the platform enforces a measurement and lapses exactly where it doesn't. Nothing was learned; something was merely counted.**

## Card template

Stories cards lean prose-heavy. Standard frontmatter (chinese, prerequisites, leads_to, tags), bilingual title, and the following sections:

> [!important] The comic rule (2026-07-17)
> **Every Story card gets at least one comic moment** — a GPT-Image panel of the story's sharpest scene, embedded at the point in the prose where the scene happens (`![[kebab-case-comic.png|560]]`). Even just an opener (a pendulum, a sneeze, a telegram) is enough. Diagrams and timelines are *maps*; the comic is the *moment* — without one, a story reads like another chapter to memorise instead of something fun, and fun is the spirit. Aim for the scene a student would retell at dinner.
>
> **Style is matched to the story's era, not uniform** — Greek red-figure vase for antiquity, Renaissance notebook ink for Galileo, Tenniel engraving for Carroll, *Puck* editorial cartoon for the 1890s, halftone silver-age comic for the space race, pixel art for the game era. Ligne claire is the *fallback*, not the house style; a style shift between panels can itself carry a joke (350 BC pottery cutting to 1971 comic print). Same-card panels should still feel like one artist unless the shift is the point.

## Tag scheme

- `type/story` — every Stories card carries this.
- `subject/mathematics`, `subject/physics`, `subject/cs` — multiple allowed for cross-domain stories.
- `era/<century>` — `era/3bc`, `era/17c`, `era/19c`, `era/20c`, etc. The era of the *events*, not the writing.
- `cast/<surname-lowercased>` — one tag per major named figure. Allows graph traversal "show me all stories featuring Newton."
- `region/<continent>` or `region/<country>` — optional, for stories where geography matters.

## Bilingual policy

Title bilingual (English / 中文). One-paragraph 中文锚点 section. Body prose can be predominantly English — Stories cards are read for the narrative, and forcing line-by-line bilingual treatment kills momentum. The Chinese anchor exists to guarantee the topic is searchable in Chinese; the body serves the story.

## Distinct from `Meta/`

`Meta/` is **methodology** — how to think across domains. The cards there ([[Chain of Thought]], [[Choosing Effective Equations]], [[Forward Reading and Problem Discovery]], [[Why Probability and Statistics]], [[Inertia and Bootstrapping]]) teach you a way of reasoning or operating.

`Stories/` is **history and human drama** — what happened, who fought whom, what survived. Stories cards don't teach a method; they tell a tale.

They cross-link freely: the Newton-Leibniz priority dispute (Story) gets cited from [[Chain of Thought]] (Meta) when discussing the role of notation in reasoning, for example. But the two folders have different jobs.
