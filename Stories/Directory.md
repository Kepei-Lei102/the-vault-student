## What this folder is for

`Mathematics/`, `Physics/`, `CS/` and `Meta/` are pedagogy-first — the maths leads, the story is incidental. **Stories/** flips it: fun-first, rigour incidental. The same way [[Heptadecagon]] uses Gauss-at-19 because the story *is* the pedagogical answer, Stories cards use story for its own sake — because how mathematics actually got made is genuinely worth knowing, and a vault that is only proofs is missing half the spirit. The vault's "fun is the spirit" north star, made structural.

## The dual-residency principle

For story-rich topics that already live in pedagogical cards (the Newton–Leibniz dispute, the discovery of $e$, Gauss at 19, the invention of variance…), the two cards share the topic:

- **The pedagogical card** keeps what is load-bearing for the maths — a compressed history that motivates the idea, plus a line and a link.
- **The Stories card** carries the full drama: primary-source quotes, context, ripples, the named figures' biographical colour.
- **They cross-link both ways.** Either card is a legitimate door into the topic.

Born-in-Stories topics (the War of the Currents, Galois's duel…) have no pedagogical residue; the story stands alone here.

## Mathematics (15)

1. **[[The Calculus Priority Dispute]]** — Newton vs Leibniz, 1665–1727: the Royal Society's rigged committee, Britain's century in the notation wilderness, Babbage's 1812 fix.
2. **[[The Hidden Number]]** — 250 years of $e$ from Napier to Hermite: "natural" means *unbidden* — five independent paths keep producing the same constant, nobody choosing it.
3. **[[The Argument for i]]** — $\sqrt{-1}$ in five acts, from Cardano's cubic through Hamilton on Brougham Bridge to Schrödinger's load-bearing $i$.
4. **[[The Bernoulli Family]]** — eight mathematicians, three generations, one feud: the L'Hôpital contract, the brachistochrone challenge to Newton, Johann's forgery against his own son.
5. **[[Stigler's Law of Eponymy]]** — no discovery is named after its discoverer, and Stigler credits Merton for the law: three mechanisms walked across ten vault eponyms.
6. **[[Lewis Carroll the Mathematician]]** — Dodgson at Christ Church: the boat trip, *Symbolic Logic*, *Pillow Problems*, and voting theory eighty years before Arrow.
7. **[[The Naming of Normal]]** — De Moivre's curve, Quetelet's average man, Galton's regression to the mean, Pearson's regretted name, and the eugenics shadow with honest edges.
8. **[[Galois at Twenty]]** — dead in a duel; the all-night letter as a will, not a discovery; the memoirs Cauchy lost and Poisson rejected; study the symmetries, not the equation.
9. **[[Abel the Other Boy Who Died Young]]** — Galois's twin, dead at twenty-six: one teacher, the six-page quintic proof Gauss never cut open, Crelle's journal, the elliptic functions.
10. **[[Inventing Variance]]** — Ceres lost and found by a jobless 24-year-old, least squares and the Legendre wound, Gauss and Laplace closing the error-law loop, Galton, Pearson, Fisher.
11. **[[Gauss the Prodigy]]** — the bricklayer's son who calculated his own birthday; the 1-to-100 legend audited, because legends, too, deserve error bars.
12. **[[The Lady Tasting Tea]]** — eight cups, seventy arrangements: Fisher inventing randomisation and the 5% line over tea, the Pearson feud, Gosset, and two honest edges.
13. **[[Russell's Paradox in the Post]]** — the 1902 letter that broke Frege's life work while Volume II sat at the printer; the prequel to Gödel.
14. **[[Ramanujan and Hardy]]** — the 1913 letter from a Madras clerk, the partition formula checked against MacMahon's hand-computed table, the taxi numbered 1729, and the notebook lost for fifty-six years.
15. **[[Erdős the Wandering Mathematician]]** — no home, one suitcase, "my brain is open"; the six-person party and the pigeonhole, the probabilistic method that proves by refusing to construct; the bet and the feud, kept difficult.

## Card template

Stories cards lean prose-heavy. Standard frontmatter (chinese, prerequisites, leads_to, tags), a bilingual title, and the sections below.

> [!important] The comic rule
> **Every Story card gets at least one comic moment** — a generated panel of the story's sharpest scene, embedded where the scene happens (`![[kebab-case-comic.png|560]]`). Even an opener (a pendulum, a sneeze, a telegram) is enough. Diagrams and timelines are *maps*; the comic is the *moment* — without one a story reads like another chapter to memorise, and fun is the spirit. Aim for the scene a student would retell at dinner.
>
> **Style is matched to the story's era, not uniform** — red-figure vase for antiquity, Renaissance notebook ink for Galileo, Tenniel engraving for Carroll, *Puck* editorial cartoon for the 1890s, halftone comic for the space race, pixel art for the game era. Ligne claire is the *fallback*, not the house style; a style shift between panels can itself carry a joke. Same-card panels should still feel like one artist unless the shift is the point.

## Tag scheme

- `type/story` — every Stories card carries this.
- `subject/mathematics`, `subject/physics`, `subject/cs` — several allowed for cross-domain stories.
- `era/<century>` — `era/3bc`, `era/17c`, `era/20c`… the era of the *events*, not the writing.
- `cast/<surname>` — one tag per major named figure, so the graph can answer "every story featuring Newton".
- `region/<place>` — optional, where geography matters.

## Bilingual policy

Bilingual title; a one-paragraph 中文锚点. The body can be predominantly English — Stories are read for the narrative, and line-by-line bilingual treatment kills momentum. The anchor guarantees the topic is searchable in Chinese; the body serves the story.

## Distinct from `Meta/`

`Meta/` is **methodology** — how to think across domains. `Stories/` is **history and human drama** — what happened, who fought whom, what survived. Stories don't teach a method; they tell a tale. The two cross-link freely but have different jobs.

## How this directory stays honest

- **One line per story.** The hook is the scene a student would retell; the card carries the acts, the receipts and the comic.
- **Counts match the disk.** The total at the top is the `.md` count in `Stories/`; the linter checks it and flags any line that has grown past a paragraph.
- **The landing story lives elsewhere.** What each story was written for, and when, is recorded in the maintainer trackers at close-out — never here.
