# Ratio 比

## Definition

A **ratio** (比) compares two or more quantities by **how many parts** each one has. "$A : B = 3 : 5$" means *for every 3 parts of $A$ there are 5 parts of $B$*. The quantities don't have to be 3 and 5 themselves — they have to be *in that proportion*: $6$ and $10$, or $12$ and $20$, or $0.6$ and $1.0$ all satisfy $3 : 5$.

A ratio is the **part-to-part** picture; a fraction is the **part-to-whole** picture of the same situation.

### 中文锚点

比 = 几个量之间"份数"的对照。$3 : 5$ 读作"三比五"；英文读 "three to five"。把 $A$ 比作 3 份，$B$ 比作 5 份，两者的比值 $\dfrac{A}{B} = \dfrac{3}{5}$。

> [!warning] Ratio vs fraction
> Ratio $3 : 5$ is **not** $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of anything. If boys and girls are in ratio $3 : 5$, then boys are $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the class (not $\dfrac{3}{5}$), because the whole class is $3 + 5 = 8$ parts. Ratio compares **part to part**; the fraction compares **part to whole**.

> [!warning] "Divide $480 in the ratio $3 : 5$" does NOT mean "divide $480 by 3 and by 5"
> It means: split $480$ into $3 + 5 = 8$ equal parts, then give $3$ parts to one person and $5$ parts to the other. One part is worth $\dfrac{480}{8} = 60$, so the shares are $180$ and $300$. This is the **unitary method** and it is examined every year.

## Simplest Form

To write a ratio in its simplest form, divide every part by the HCF.

| Ratio | Working | Simplest form |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| $12 : 8$ | HCF $= 4$ | $3 : 2$ |
| $6 : 12 : 18$ | HCF $= 6$ | $1 : 2 : 3$ |
| $0.4 : 0.6$ | multiply by 10 | $2 : 3$ |
| $\tfrac{1}{2} : \tfrac{2}{3}$ | multiply by 6 | $3 : 4$ |

Two special forms examiners ask for: **unit form** $1 : n$ — divide both sides by the first quantity (used in scale problems like "$1$ cm represents $50$ km", a scale of $1 : 5{,}000{,}000$); **whole-number form** — multiply through to clear decimals and fractions.

## Divide in a Ratio — The Unitary Method

To divide a quantity $Q$ in the ratio $a : b$ (or $a : b : c$):

$$\text{value of one part} = \frac{Q}{a + b \; (+ c \; \ldots)}, \qquad \text{then multiply by } a, b, c, \ldots$$

**Worked template.** "Divide \$960 in the ratio $3 : 4 : 5$."
Total parts = $3 + 4 + 5 = 12$. One part = $\dfrac{960}{12} = 80$. Shares = $3 \times 80 = 240$, $4 \times 80 = 320$, $5 \times 80 = 400$. Check: $240 + 320 + 400 = 960$. ✓

> [!tip] Exam phrasing — "in the ratio"
> The phrase *"in the ratio $3 : 5$"* always signals the unitary method. Total the parts, find one part, multiply. Chinese students often divide $Q$ by one of the numbers directly — that gives the wrong answer unless the ratio is $1 : n$.

> [!tip] Exam phrasing — finding one quantity from another
> *"$A : B = 3 : 5$. If $A = 15$, find $B$."* — one part is $\dfrac{15}{3} = 5$, so $B = 5 \times 5 = 25$. The unitary method again, just with one share already given.

## Fraction Link

Any ratio $a : b$ can be rewritten as a fraction, but you must decide *what the whole is*:

| Statement | Fractional meaning |
|-----------|--------------------|
| "$A : B = 3 : 5$" | $\dfrac{A}{B} = \dfrac{3}{5}$ (part to part) |
| "$A$ is $\dfrac{?}{?}$ of the total" | $\dfrac{A}{A+B} = \dfrac{3}{8}$ (part to whole) |
| "$B$ is $\dfrac{?}{?}$ of the total" | $\dfrac{B}{A+B} = \dfrac{5}{8}$ |

## Exam Notes

### OxAQA 9260
**Syllabus ref:** N16 (simplest form and notation), N17 (divide in a ratio). Almost every 9260 paper has a ratio-division word problem, often with money, recipes, or three-part shares. Command words: *simplify*, *divide*, *share*, *express in the form*.

### Cambridge 0580 Extended
**Syllabus ref:** E1.11 (ratio, simplifying, dividing, including three-part). Identical treatment to 9260 but often combined with percentages or fractions in the same question.

### Cambridge 0606
Ratios underpin enlargement scale factors and are assumed knowledge; not directly examined as "ratio problems."
