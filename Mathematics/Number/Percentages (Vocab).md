# Percentages 百分数

## Definition

A **percentage** (百分数) is a fraction with denominator 100, written with the sign **%**. $25\%$ means $\dfrac{25}{100}$, or "25 out of every 100," or the decimal $0.25$. The word comes from the Latin *per centum* — "per hundred" — the direct translation of 百分 ("of a hundred parts").

### 中文锚点

百分数 = 以 100 为分母的分数。$25\% = \dfrac{25}{100} = 0.25$。中文读作"百分之二十五"（分母先说），英文读作"twenty-five percent"（分子先说）—— 顺序相反，和分数一样。

> [!warning] Reading order: English vs Chinese
> English says **percent** (top first): "twenty-five percent." Chinese says **百分之…** (bottom first): "百分之二十五." Same number, opposite word order. Identical to the fraction reading convention.

## Key Vocabulary

| English | 中文 | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| percent / per cent | 百分之… (bǎi fēn zhī) | The sign is **%**; UK spells "per cent", US spells "percent" |
| percentage | 百分数 / 百分比 (bǎifēnbǐ) | The quantity itself; always written with % |
| percentage point | 百分点 | Different from "percent"! See callout below |
| express as a percentage | 用百分数表示 | Exam command — convert $\frac{17}{20}$ to $85\%$ |
| **of** (a number) | ……的 | "$25\%$ **of** 80" = $0.25 \times 80$ — "of" means multiply |
| proportion | 比例 / 占比 | The part-to-whole relationship, often given as a % |

> [!warning] "Percent" vs "percentage point"
> If a tax rate rises from **$20\%$ to $25\%$**, that is a **5 percentage-point** increase — but a **$25\%$** percentage increase (because $\frac{5}{20} = 25\%$). Exam questions occasionally test this distinction, especially in stats and economics contexts.

## Converting Between Forms

Fractions, decimals, and percentages are three ways of writing the same quantity. Convert freely:

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage | English phrasing |
|----------|---------|------------|------------------|
| $\dfrac{1}{2}$ | $0.5$ | $50\%$ | "one half" / "fifty percent" |
| $\dfrac{1}{4}$ | $0.25$ | $25\%$ | "a quarter" / "twenty-five percent" |
| $\dfrac{3}{4}$ | $0.75$ | $75\%$ | "three quarters" / "seventy-five percent" |
| $\dfrac{1}{5}$ | $0.2$ | $20\%$ | "a fifth" / "twenty percent" |
| $\dfrac{7}{20}$ | $0.35$ | $35\%$ | "seven twentieths" |
| $\dfrac{5}{4}$ | $1.25$ | $125\%$ | "one and a quarter" / "one hundred and twenty-five percent" |

**Conversion rules.** Fraction → percentage: multiply by $100\%$. Decimal → percentage: shift two places right ($0.45 \to 45\%$). Percentage → decimal: shift two places left ($72\% \to 0.72$). Percentage → fraction: put over 100 and simplify.

## Percentage as Operator

"$X\%$ of $Y$" is an **operator** on $Y$: it scales $Y$ by the factor $\dfrac{X}{100}$.

$$X\% \text{ of } Y \;=\; \frac{X}{100} \times Y$$

| English phrase | Calculation |
|----------------|-------------|
| "$20\%$ of $150$" | $0.20 \times 150 = 30$ |
| "Find $8\%$ of $\$250$" | $0.08 \times 250 = \$20$ |
| "What percentage of $40$ is $14$?" | $\dfrac{14}{40} \times 100\% = 35\%$ |
| "Express $17$ as a percentage of $20$" | $\dfrac{17}{20} \times 100\% = 85\%$ |

> [!tip] Exam phrasing — "express X as a percentage of Y"
> The target is always **Y** (the whole). The quantity **X** goes on top. $\dfrac{X}{Y} \times 100\%$. Students sometimes divide the wrong way — remember: *of* names the whole, *as a percentage of* names the denominator.

## Percentages Above 100%

A percentage can exceed $100\%$. $150\%$ of $40$ is $60$; $300\%$ of $x$ is $3x$. This appears whenever a quantity has grown beyond its original value, or when a ratio is greater than $1 : 1$. $100\%$ of $Y$ is just $Y$ itself.

> [!warning] "Increase by $100\%$" vs "is $100\%$ of"
> "Increase **by** $100\%$" means **double** (add the whole again, so the new value is $200\%$ of the old). "Is $100\%$ **of**" just means the full original quantity. English exam questions use the preposition *by* for the change and *of/to* for the final state.
