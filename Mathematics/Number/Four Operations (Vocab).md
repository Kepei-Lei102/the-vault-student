# Four Operations 四则运算

## Definition

The **four operations** are addition ($+$), subtraction ($-$), multiplication ($\times$), and division ($\div$). These are the basic operations of arithmetic.

### 中文锚点

三个朋友各带了两块饼干，桌上一共有多少？你可以一堆一堆地加，也可以直接说“三份两块”——乘法把同样大小的一份重复几次，记成了一步。再把这六块平均分回三个人手里，除法就把刚才的分组倒着走了一遍。运算符不是催你动笔的口令：先想清楚这些饼干怎样合在一起、怎样分开，你才知道算式该怎么写。

## Key Vocabulary

### Ways to say each operation

| Operation | Verb forms | Result noun | 中文 |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|------|
| $+$ Addition | add, plus, sum, increase by, more than, total | **sum** 和 (hé) | 加 (jiā) |
| $-$ Subtraction | subtract, minus, take away, decrease by, less than, reduce by | **difference** 差 (chā) | 减 (jiǎn) |
| $\times$ Multiplication | multiply, times, lots of, of (as in "$\dfrac{1}{3}$ of 12") | **product** 积 (jī) | 乘 (chéng) |
| $\div$ Division | divide, share, split, how many times, ratio | **quotient** 商 (shāng) | 除 (chú) |

> [!warning] "Less than" vs "is less than"
> "5 **less than** $x$" means $x - 5$ (subtraction). "$x$ **is less than** 5" means $x < 5$ (inequality). One is an operation, the other is a comparison. This trips up students constantly.

### Other vocabulary

| English | 中文 | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| remainder | 余数 (yúshù) | Left over after integer division: 17 ÷ 5 = 3 remainder 2 |
| fraction | 分数 (fēnshù) | A number written as $\dfrac{a}{b}$, with $b\ne0$ |
| proper fraction | 真分数 (zhēn fēnshù) | For non-negative numerator and positive denominator: numerator < denominator, e.g. $\dfrac{3}{7}$ |
| improper fraction | 假分数 (jiǎ fēnshù) | For non-negative numerator and positive denominator: numerator ≥ denominator, e.g. $\dfrac{9}{4}$ |
| mixed number | 带分数 (dài fēnshù) | Whole part + fraction, e.g. $2\dfrac{1}{4}$ |
| reciprocal | 倒数 (dàoshù) | Flip the fraction: reciprocal of $\dfrac{3}{5}$ is $\dfrac{5}{3}$. Reciprocal of nonzero $n$ is $\dfrac{1}{n}$ |
| numerator | 分子 (fēnzǐ) | Top of a fraction |
| denominator | 分母 (fēnmǔ) | Bottom of a fraction |

> [!tip] Exam phrasing
> "Find the **product** of $x$ and $y$" means multiply. "Find the **sum** of..." means add. These words appear in 9260 and 0580 without further explanation — you are expected to know them.

## Worked example — read the grouping first

Evaluate $-6-2\times(-3)$.

**Trigger:** multiplication sits inside a subtraction. **Tool:** operation order, then the multiplication sign rule. First $2\times(-3)=-6$; then $-6-(-6)=0$. [[Integers]] proves why removing a negative adds its opposite.

## Exam Notes

### Cambridge 0580 — Core and Extended

**C1.6/E1.6** (2025–27) requires the four operations on integers, fractions and decimals, including negative numbers, operation order and practical situations. Papers 1/2 are non-calculator; Papers 3/4 allow calculators. Operation vocabulary belongs to both tiers.

### OxfordAQA 9260

**N2** requires the four operations with positive and negative integers, decimals and fractions, including contexts. **N3** requires inverse operations and operation order. Both papers permit calculators; a calculator does not replace identifying the requested operation.

### Later courses and scope

Cambridge 0606 and 9709 assume IGCSE-level mathematics; 9231 builds on 9709. Pearson Edexcel IAL, OxfordAQA 9660 and AP Calculus AB/BC use this arithmetic throughout their algebra. This is a mathematical dependency, not a new stand-alone advanced topic or an entry requirement: 9660 explicitly imposes no previous-learning requirement.

IB AA and AI (first assessment 2021, SL/HL) explicitly list operations on integers, decimals and fractions, including operation order, as prior learning assumed in examination questions. [AA guide](https://ibo.org/globalassets/new-structure/university-admission/pdfs/dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-guide-en.pdf) · [AI guide](https://ibo.org/globalassets/new-structure/university-admission/pdfs/dp-mathematics-applications-and-interpretation-guide-en.pdf).

No advanced stand-alone construction of the operations is prescribed by these specifications. The arithmetic and its interpretation remain examinable within their relevant questions.
