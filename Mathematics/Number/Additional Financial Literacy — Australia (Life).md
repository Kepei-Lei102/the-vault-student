# Additional Financial Literacy — Australia 澳大利亚金融常识

## What this card is

A **life card** supplementing [[Financial Literacy (Life)]]. For the student arriving in Australia. The Australian system has three pieces that no other country does quite the same way:

1. **Superannuation** — compulsory retirement saving, employer-funded at 11.5% of wages. If you work here, you have a super account whether you asked for one or not.
2. **HECS-HELP** — the student loan, which is *CPI-indexed rather than interest-bearing* and repaid via the tax system.
3. **Franking credits** — a dividend-imputation system that makes Australian share investing mechanically different from anywhere else.

And one piece of healthcare / insurance arithmetic every arriving student must plan around: **Lifetime Health Cover loading** — buy private health insurance before your 31st birthday or pay a permanent surcharge.

### 中文锚点

澳洲三个全球独特的制度：**超级年金 (Super)** — 雇主强制 11.5% 供款；**HECS-HELP** — 学生贷款按 CPI 指数化 (不计利息), 通过税务系统归还；**分红抵税 (Franking Credits)** — 上市公司已纳的公司税可以抵个人所得税。此外还有 **终身医保加载 (LHC Loading)** — 31 岁前买私人医保，否则终身附加 2%/年。

> [!info] Numbers in this card are 2024–2026
> Super Guarantee rate, HECS-HELP indexation, and Medicare Levy thresholds change annually. Values here are accurate as of early 2026; check ato.gov.au and yourMoney.gov.au for current figures.

## 1. Banking

### Account types

| English | 中文 | What it is |
|---------|------|-----------|
| Transaction account | 活期账户 | = US checking / UK current; daily spending, debit card |
| Savings account | 储蓄账户 | Often with "bonus interest" conditional on deposits / no withdrawals |
| Term deposit | 定期存款 | = US CD / 定期; fixed term, early-withdrawal penalty |
| Offset account | 抵减账户 | Special transaction account linked to a mortgage — balance *offsets* the mortgage balance for interest calculation. See §6 |

### Key protections and identifiers

**Financial Claims Scheme (FCS)** — Australian Government guarantee covering **up to AU$250,000 per person per ADI (Authorised Deposit-taking Institution)**. Equivalent to FDIC / FSCS.

**BSB + account number** — Australian banks use a **6-digit BSB (Bank-State-Branch)** code plus an account number. Both are visible on the card and statement. You need both to receive a transfer.

### Transfers and payments

| Method | Speed | Cost | Use case |
|--------|-------|------|----------|
| PayID / Osko | Seconds | Free | Real-time transfer using phone number or email instead of BSB |
| Direct debit | 1–3 days | Free | Recurring bill payments (utilities, subscriptions) |
| BPAY | Same day | Free | Pay a biller by reference number (utilities, rates) |
| EFTPOS | Instant | Free | Domestic debit card network; accepted everywhere |
| Tap-to-pay / PayWave | Instant | Free | Contactless under AU$200 without PIN |

## 2. Superannuation — the one you can't opt out of

**Superannuation ("super")** is Australia's compulsory retirement-saving system. If an employer pays you wages, they **must** pay an additional percentage into a super fund *on top of* your salary. This is the **Superannuation Guarantee (SG)**.

| Year | SG rate |
|------|---------|
| 2024–25 | 11.5% |
| 2025–26 | 12.0% |
| 2026+ | 12.0% (plateau) |

The money is yours, locked until you reach **preservation age** (60 for anyone born after July 1964) and meet a condition of release (usually retirement). Before then you cannot access it except in narrow hardship cases — or, for temporary visa holders leaving Australia, via the **Departing Australia Superannuation Payment (DASP)**, which lets you withdraw your super but with a heavy withholding tax (35–65% depending on category).

### Concessional vs non-concessional contributions

| Type | 中文 | What it is | Annual cap (2024–25) | Tax in super |
|------|------|-----------|----------------------:|--------------|
| Concessional | 税前供款 | Employer SG + salary sacrifice + tax-deductible personal contributions | $30,000 | 15% |
| Non-concessional | 税后供款 | After-tax personal contributions | $120,000 | 0% |

**Salary sacrifice** lets you redirect pre-tax salary into super. For a higher-rate taxpayer, this saves the difference between their marginal rate and the 15% super tax — often 22–30% on each dollar sacrificed.

### Finding your super

If you've worked in Australia before and lost track of an account — common for international students with casual jobs — check **myGov → ATO → Super** to see all accounts in your name and consolidate. Lost super is a common money sink (multiple accounts, multiple sets of fees).

### SMSF

A **Self-Managed Super Fund** lets you manage your own super investments directly (typical for business owners or high-net-worth individuals). Administration is heavy — not relevant for most students.

## 3. Taxes

### Identifiers and how tax works

- **TFN (Tax File Number)** — 8–9 digit unique identifier, issued by the ATO. Required to work (without it, employers must withhold at the top marginal rate), open interest-bearing accounts, or invest. Apply online once you have a visa.
- **ATO (Australian Taxation Office)** — the federal tax authority.
- **PAYG (Pay As You Go)** — employer withholds tax from each paycheck, you reconcile via annual tax return.

### Income tax rates (2024–25, residents)

| Bracket | Rate |
|---------|------|
| $0 – $18,200 | 0% |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 16% |
| $45,001 – $135,000 | 30% |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | 37% |
| $190,001 + | 45% |

Plus a **2% Medicare Levy** on most resident taxpayers. Non-residents pay from the first dollar (no tax-free threshold).

### Medicare Levy and Medicare Levy Surcharge

- **Medicare Levy** — 2% of taxable income, funds Medicare. Most residents pay it.
- **Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS)** — additional **1–1.5%** for higher earners (single > ~$97k, family > ~$194k in 2024–25) who **don't hold appropriate private hospital cover**. MLS is designed to push high earners onto private insurance to relieve pressure on Medicare.

> [!tip] The MLS math
> A single taxpayer earning $110,000 pays ~$1,100/year in MLS if they don't have private hospital cover. A basic private policy costs ~$1,200/year. So the policy is often nearly free for higher-income earners after the MLS saving. Plug the numbers for your own income before deciding.

### Franking credits — unique to Australia

Australia uses a **dividend imputation** system. When an Australian company pays corporate tax (30%) on its profits and distributes the remainder as a **fully franked dividend**, the shareholder receives both:

1. The cash dividend, and
2. A **franking credit** equal to the corporate tax already paid.

At tax time, the shareholder declares the dividend *plus* the franking credit as income, and the franking credit offsets their personal tax. If the credit exceeds the tax owed, the ATO **refunds** the excess — a uniquely Australian feature.

Worked example: ASX company declares a $70 fully franked dividend per share. The corporate tax already paid was $30 per share (30% of the $100 pre-tax profit).

- The shareholder reports **$100 of taxable income** (grossed up)
- They receive a **$30 franking credit** against their tax
- If their marginal rate is 30%, net tax = $0 (the credit covers it exactly)
- If their marginal rate is 19%, the ATO **refunds** the $11 excess credit

This is why Australian retirees hold Australian shares heavily — the franking credits can generate a negative effective tax rate. Foreign investors cannot use franking credits.

## 4. HECS-HELP — the student loan

**HECS-HELP** (Higher Education Contribution Scheme — Higher Education Loan Program) is how Australian citizens and permanent residents pay for domestic university tuition. **It is not a conventional debt.**

### How it works

- Tuition is loaned by the government; no payment required while studying.
- **No interest is charged.** The balance is *indexed to CPI* each 1 June — so it rises with inflation but not faster.
- Repayment is **income-contingent** and collected via the tax system. Your employer withholds HECS repayment along with tax when your income exceeds the threshold.

### Repayment thresholds (2024–25)

| Income band | Repayment rate |
|------------:|---------------:|
| Below $54,435 | 0% |
| $54,435 – $62,850 | 1% |
| $62,851 – $66,620 | 2% |
| … scaling up … | … |
| $159,664 + | 10% |

There is no fixed term or write-off like the UK — the debt persists until repaid. But because it tracks CPI (not interest), the **real** value of the debt does not grow.

### International students

International students **cannot** access HECS-HELP. Domestic fees under HECS-HELP are typically AU$10,000–15,000/year; international tuition is typically AU$35,000–60,000/year, paid directly and without loan support.

## 5. Healthcare — Medicare plus private health

### Medicare — the public system

All Australian citizens, permanent residents, and some visa holders are covered by **Medicare**, the universal public health insurance, funded by the **Medicare Levy** (§3). Medicare covers:

- GP visits (fully covered if the doctor **bulk-bills**; otherwise a gap fee applies)
- Public hospital treatment (free, but with waiting lists for elective surgery)
- Subsidised prescriptions via the **PBS (Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme)**
- A schedule fee for specialist care (specialists often charge above the fee; the gap is yours)

### Private health insurance

Optional top-up; used for faster access to elective surgery, a private room, and your choice of specialist. Two separate components:

- **Hospital cover** — for inpatient care
- **Extras cover** — for dental, optical, physio, etc. (NOT covered by Medicare)

### Lifetime Health Cover (LHC) loading — the 31st-birthday rule

This is **the one insurance rule every Australian over 30 knows by heart**. If you don't take out **hospital cover** before **1 July after your 31st birthday**, you pay a **2% loading** for every year you delay, added permanently to your premium once you do buy cover (capped at 70%).

| Age you first take cover | Loading |
|-------------------------:|--------:|
| By 31 | **0%** |
| 35 | 10% |
| 40 | 20% |
| 50 | 40% |
| 60 | 70% (cap) |

Loading is **removed after 10 years of continuous cover** — but if you drop cover, the clock resets. Designed as a stick to push everyone into private insurance while they're young and cheap to insure.

### Private Health Insurance rebate

Residents below certain income thresholds get a **partial rebate** (15–33%) on their private health premiums, paid either as a lower premium or as a tax offset. Progressively reduced for higher earners.

## 6. Property and Mortgages

### Mortgage structure — very different from the US

The Australian mortgage default is **variable-rate** — the opposite of the US. Fixed-rate options exist (usually 1–5 years) but most mortgages track the lender's variable rate, which moves with the Reserve Bank's cash rate.

### The offset account — Australia's signature mortgage feature

An **offset account** is a regular transaction account **linked to your mortgage**. Its balance is **subtracted from the mortgage principal** when interest is calculated — so $50,000 in offset against a $500,000 mortgage means you only pay interest on $450,000.

Economic effect: the offset balance earns the **mortgage rate** (e.g. 6%) risk-free, after-tax. No interest income is declared (you didn't earn interest — you *avoided* paying it). Used as the primary cash-parking place for anyone with a mortgage.

### Redraw

Extra repayments you make can be **redrawn** later if you need the cash. Redraw and offset are economically similar but taxed and treated differently — offset is more flexible; redraw has more restrictions in some products.

### Negative gearing

If the cost of holding an investment property (mortgage interest + maintenance + depreciation) **exceeds** the rental income, the loss is **deductible against other income** — including salary. This is "negative gearing" and is the main tax reason Australians hold investment property. Combined with the **50% CGT discount** on assets held over 12 months, it has been a major political topic for decades.

### Stamp duty

Property purchase tax, paid by the buyer, varying by **state**. Rates are progressive and vary significantly — NSW and Victoria tend to be the highest. First-home-buyer concessions exist in every state but apply under different price caps. Overseas buyers pay surcharges.

## 7. Buy Now, Pay Later

Australia is the birthplace of the modern **Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL)** model. Afterpay (ASX: APT until acquired by Square/Block in 2022), Zip, and Klarna all operate here. Standard structure:

- Split a purchase into 4 equal fortnightly payments
- No interest charged *if paid on time*
- Late fees apply if missed
- Merchants pay the BNPL provider 4–6% per transaction (hidden in the retail price)

BNPL is increasingly treated as credit by regulators — expect more disclosure requirements and credit-check integration from 2025 onward. For students: it looks interest-free and is, if you never miss; miss once and the effective rate spikes.

## 8. International-Student Corner

### Visa and work limits

- **Student visa (subclass 500)** — allows full-time study.
- Work rights: **up to 48 hours per fortnight** during term (2024 rule, up from earlier caps), unlimited during scheduled breaks.
- After course completion: eligibility for a **Post-Study Work visa (subclass 485)**, 2–4 years depending on qualification and region.

### OSHC — mandatory health cover

**Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)** is **compulsory** for student-visa holders and must be maintained for the entire visa period. Typical cost: AU$600–800/year for a single student. Covers hospital, doctor visits, some pharmaceuticals. Medicare is **not** available to international students (except for citizens of countries with reciprocal agreements — no such agreement with China).

### TFN and super as an international student

- Apply for a **TFN** once you arrive, via the ATO website — takes about 10 business days.
- If you take any paid work, your employer pays **Superannuation Guarantee** on your behalf (11.5% of wages). This accumulates in a super account in your name.
- When you leave Australia permanently, you can claim it back via **DASP (Departing Australia Superannuation Payment)** — but a withholding tax applies (35% for most categories, 65% for certain working-holiday-maker earnings). Worth claiming; worth also knowing the haircut.

### Banking as a new arrival

The "Big Four" (CommBank, Westpac, ANZ, NAB) all have international-student onboarding programs — you can open an account online **before arrival** with passport + visa + a deposit. Fintech (UP, Bank of Melbourne, Macquarie) is strong in Australia and often has better features.

### Casual, part-time, full-time

Australian labour market distinguishes three statuses:

- **Casual** — no paid leave; 25% "casual loading" on hourly rate compensates; can be terminated freely
- **Part-time** — fixed weekly hours, accrues leave pro-rata
- **Full-time** — 38 hours/week, accrues leave, standard protections

Students typically work casual. Know your hourly rate including loading, and know which award (industry agreement) covers your job — award rates are minimums below which employers cannot go, regardless of what they tell you.
