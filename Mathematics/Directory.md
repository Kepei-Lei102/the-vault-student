2. **[[Trigonometric Functions]]** — Three views (ratio, unit circle, wave), CAST diagram, symmetry properties, $y = a\sin(b\theta + c) + d$ transformations

4. **[[Radians]]** — Deep card: arc/radius definition, conversion $\pi \leftrightarrow 180°$, arc length $s = r\theta$, sector area $\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta$, the squeeze-theorem proof that $\sin h / h \to 1$ and why calculus demands radians, small-angle approximations
5. **[[Trigonometric Identities]]** — Deep card: Pythagorean trio with three derivations (unit-circle, right-triangle, power-series); reciprocal/quotient/even-odd/co-function identities; sum/difference + double-angle (A-Level/IB/AP); four standard identity-proof tactics with worked examples (§10.6 skill); two-panel unit-circle SVG showing both Pythagorean identities geometrically; closes 0606 §10.4 + §10.6
6. **[[Trigonometric Equations]]** — Deep card: the reduce-to-standard-form template (one trig of one argument = c); CAST-diagram-derived "second solution per period" rules (π−x₀ for sin, 2π−x₀ for cos, x₀+π for tan); four families with worked examples (linear, quadratic-in-trig, Pythagorean reduction, multiple-angle with the domain-transformation trap); the "don't divide by sin x" warning; R-sin form for $a\sin x + b\cos x$ (A-Level/IB); three-panel unit-circle SVG showing why two solutions per period geometrically; closes 0606 §10.5
7. **[[Trigonometric Graphs]]** — Deep card: three parent graphs (sin, cos, tan) with key features; the three transformations of $y = a\sin(bx)+c$ — amplitude $|a|$, period $2\pi/b$ (sin/cos) or $\pi/b$ (tan), vertical shift $c$; sketching procedure; reverse-problem extraction of (a, b, c) from a given graph; tan's asymptote-driven differences (no amplitude, period $\pi$); three SVGs (parents, transformations, worked example $y = 3\sin 2x - 1$); closes 0606 §10.2 + §10.3 — final two rows of §10 cluster

## Exam Strategy / Formula Sheet References

- **[[MF19 Reference (9709)]]** — the Cambridge MF19 booklet, free during the exam; what's given vs. what to memorise across Papers 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6. (Paper 2 is the AS-only route; Paper 3 is the A Level one.)
- **[[MF19 Reference (9231)]]** — the *same booklet's* Further pages, audited for 9231: Further Pure, Further Mechanics, Further Prob & Stats, and the $t$ / $\chi^2$ / Wilcoxon $T$ / Wilcoxon $W$ tables. The headline finding is the asymmetry — **Paper 1 gets three summation results and a scalar product; Paper 4 gets four statistical tables**, because MF19 prints numbers nobody could compute by hand and withholds anything derivable. Also records what is absent booklet-wide (vector product, de Moivre, every matrix formula, the polar sector integral, $\chi^2$'s own statistic).
- **[[AP Calculus Reference]]** — AP Calculus AB and BC. *No formula sheet provided on exam day*; full memorise-list across foundational + inverse-trig + special-trig + BC series + parametric / polar / vector. ~70 distinct items at recall speed.
- **[[Edexcel IAL Reference]]** — Pearson Edexcel International A-Level. Booklet provided, less generous than MF19 (no inverse-trig integrals on Pure-only papers — those live on FP3) but more generous than AP. Cumulative-inheritance rule: P4 candidates also get P1+P2+P3 contents. *Big surprise:* Edexcel IAL gives $\int \sec x$ and $\int \csc x$ in two equivalent forms, where Cambridge MF19 makes 9709 students derive them.
- **[[OxAQA 9660 Reference]]** — Oxford International AQA International A-Level. **The most generous formula booklet of any major international A-Level board.** Gives $\sin^{-1}, \cos^{-1}, \tan^{-1}$ derivatives, the full inverse-trig + inverse-hyperbolic integral table, all hyperbolic-function derivatives, $\int \sec x$ + $\int \csc x$ in two forms, *and* 3D rotation matrices — all in standard Pure (no Further Math required). A 9660 student's must-memorise list is ~15-20 items; an AP BC student's is ~70.
