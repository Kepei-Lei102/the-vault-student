# Quadrilaterals 四边形

## Definition

A **quadrilateral** (四边形) is a polygon with exactly four sides and four angles. The interior angle sum is always $360°$ (because any quadrilateral splits into two triangles: $2 \times 180° = 360°$).

### 中文锚点

四边形 = 四条边的多边形。英文名称和中文有些差异：梯形在英式英语是 trapezium，美式是 trapezoid（正好反过来）。考试用英式。"平行四边形"不是"长方形"——长方形多了"四个直角"的条件。

---

## Key Vocabulary — The Family Tree

Each shape below is a **special case** of the ones above it. Moving down adds constraints.

| English | 中文 | Defining property |
|---------|------|-------------------|
| quadrilateral | 四边形 | Any 4-sided polygon |
| trapezium (UK) | 梯形 (tīxíng) | Exactly **one** pair of parallel sides |
| parallelogram | 平行四边形 (píngxíng sìbiānxíng) | **Two** pairs of parallel sides |
| rectangle | 长方形 / 矩形 (jǔxíng) | Parallelogram with **four right angles** |
| rhombus | 菱形 (língxíng) | Parallelogram with **four equal sides** |
| square | 正方形 (zhèngfāngxíng) | Rectangle + rhombus: four right angles AND four equal sides |
| kite | 风筝形 / 鹞形 (yàoxíng) | **Two** pairs of adjacent equal sides (not opposite) |

> [!warning] Trapezium vs trapezoid — a transatlantic trap
> In **British English** (used by Cambridge and OxAQA): **trapezium** = one pair of parallel sides. In **American English**: **trapezoid** = one pair of parallel sides, and "trapezium" means no parallel sides at all. The meanings are **swapped**. For 9260/0580, always use the British definition.

### Properties at a glance

| Shape | Parallel sides | Equal sides | Diagonals | Angles |
|-------|---------------|-------------|-----------|--------|
| Parallelogram | 2 pairs | Opposite pairs equal | Bisect each other | Opposite equal |
| Rectangle | 2 pairs | Opposite pairs equal | Bisect each other, **equal length** | All $90°$ |
| Rhombus | 2 pairs | **All four** equal | Bisect each other **at right angles** | Opposite equal |
| Square | 2 pairs | All four equal | Bisect at right angles, equal length | All $90°$ |
| Kite | 0 | 2 adjacent pairs | One bisects the other **at right angles** | One pair of opposite angles equal |
| Trapezium | 1 pair | Not necessarily | No special rule | Co-interior angles between parallels sum to $180°$ |

> [!tip] WHY the hierarchy matters
> "Every square is a rectangle" but not every rectangle is a square. The hierarchy means that every property of a parallelogram is automatically inherited by rectangles, rhombuses, and squares. If a question says "ABCD is a parallelogram," you immediately know opposite sides are equal, opposite angles are equal, and diagonals bisect each other — even if the diagram looks like a rectangle.

## Key Terms

| English | 中文 | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| diagonal | 对角线 (duìjiǎoxiàn) | Line segment connecting non-adjacent vertices |
| bisect | 平分 (píngfēn) | Cut exactly in half |
| perpendicular bisector | 垂直平分线 | Cuts in half at $90°$ |
| adjacent | 相邻的 (xiānglín de) | Next to each other (sharing a vertex) |
| opposite | 对面的 (duìmiàn de) | Across from each other |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Saying "a square is not a rectangle."** It is — a square is a special rectangle where all sides are also equal. The hierarchy is inclusive.
2. **Confusing kite and rhombus.** A rhombus has opposite sides equal and parallel. A kite has adjacent sides equal and is generally not a parallelogram (unless it's a rhombus).
3. **Using "trapezoid" in a Cambridge exam.** Use "trapezium" — the British convention.
