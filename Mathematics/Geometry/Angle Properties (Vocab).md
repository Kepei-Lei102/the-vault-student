# Angle Properties 角的性质

## Definition

The fundamental angle relationships that follow from straight lines and intersecting lines. These are the building blocks for every geometry proof from here to Circle Theorems.

### 中文锚点

角的性质 = 直线和交线产生的角度关系。互补 = 加起来 $180°$，互余 = 加起来 $90°$，对顶角 = 相等。

---

## Key Vocabulary

| English                       | 中文                | Definition                                     |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **acute angle**               | 锐角 (ruìjiǎo)      | $0° < \theta < 90°$ — yes, it IS "a cute angle" 🥰 (可爱的角) |
| **right angle**               | 直角 (zhíjiǎo)      | Exactly $90°$; marked with a small square      |
| **obtuse angle**              | 钝角 (dùnjiǎo)      | $90° < \theta < 180°$                          |
| **straight angle**            | 平角 (píngjiǎo)     | Exactly $180°$; a straight line                |
| **reflex angle**              | 优角 (yōujiǎo)      | $180° < \theta < 360°$                         |
| **complementary**             | 互余 (hùyú)         | Two angles that sum to $90°$                   |
| **supplementary**             | 互补 (hùbǔ)         | Two angles that sum to $180°$                  |
| **vertically opposite**       | 对顶角 (duìdǐngjiǎo) | Formed by two intersecting lines; always equal |
| **angles on a straight line** | 直线上的角             | Sum to $180°$                                  |
| **angles at a point**         | 一点周围的角            | Sum to $360°$                                  |

> [!warning] Complementary vs supplementary — don't mix them up
> **C**omplementary = **C**orner ($90°$). **S**upplementary = **S**traight ($180°$). If you can remember "C before S" and "$90°$ before $180°$", you'll never confuse them.

---

## The Three Core Facts

### 1. Angles on a straight line sum to $180°$

**WHY.** A straight line is a $180°$ angle. Any angles that partition it must add up to $180°$.

### 2. Angles at a point sum to $360°$

**WHY.** A full rotation is $360°$. Any angles that partition the rotation must add up to $360°$.

### 3. Vertically opposite angles are equal

**WHY.** When two lines cross, they make four angles. Call adjacent angles $a$ and $b$. Since $a + b = 180°$ (straight line) and $b + c = 180°$ (the other straight line), we get $a = c$. The opposite angles must be equal — not by definition, but by proof.

## Exam Notes

**9260 G2 / 0580 E4.1.** "Calculate unknown angles using the properties of angles at a point, on a straight line, and vertically opposite angles."

**Typical phrasing:** "Find the value of $x$, giving reasons for each step." The word **"reasons"** means you must name the property: "angles on a straight line," "vertically opposite angles," etc. Just writing the number scores fewer marks.

## Beyond the Syllabus

**Angle measurement is arbitrary.** Degrees ($360°$ per circle) come from the Babylonians' base-60 system. Radians ($2\pi$ per circle) are the natural unit for calculus (see [[Trigonometric Functions]]). Gradians ($400$ per circle) are used in French surveying. The *relationships* (supplementary, vertically opposite) hold regardless of the unit.
