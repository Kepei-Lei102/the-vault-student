# Triangles 三角形

## Definition

A **triangle** (三角形) is a polygon with exactly three sides and three angles. The angle sum is always $180°$ — this is proven from [[Angles in Parallel Lines (Vocab)]] (draw a line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side).

### 中文锚点

三角形分类有两套体系：按边分（等边、等腰、不等边）和按角分（锐角、直角、钝角）。英文考试常考 "isosceles" 和 "scalene" 的拼写和性质。

## Key Vocabulary

| English | 中文 | Definition |
|---------|------|------------|
| triangle | 三角形 (sānjiǎoxíng) | Three-sided polygon |
| scalene | 不等边三角形 (bùděngbiān) | All three sides different, all three angles different |
| isosceles | 等腰三角形 (děngyāo) | Two equal sides; the angles opposite them (base angles) are also equal |
| equilateral | 等边三角形 (děngbiān) | All three sides equal; all angles $= 60°$ |
| right-angled | 直角三角形 (zhíjiǎo) | One angle is exactly $90°$ |
| hypotenuse | 斜边 (xiébiān) | The side opposite the right angle — always the longest side |
| base | 底边 (dǐbiān) | Any side chosen as the "bottom" for area calculations |
| height / altitude | 高 (gāo) | Perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite vertex |
| vertex (pl. vertices) | 顶点 (dǐngdiǎn) | A corner of the triangle |
| interior angle | 内角 (nèijiǎo) | Angle inside the triangle; three interior angles sum to $180°$ |
| exterior angle | 外角 (wàijiǎo) | Angle formed by one side and the extension of an adjacent side; equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles |

---

## Classification — Two Independent Systems

Triangles are classified by **sides** and by **angles** independently. A triangle gets one label from each system:

| By sides | By angles |
|----------|-----------|
| Scalene (all different) | Acute (all angles $< 90°$) |
| Isosceles (two equal) | Right (one angle $= 90°$) |
| Equilateral (all equal) | Obtuse (one angle $> 90°$) |

So you can have a "right isosceles triangle" (the 45-45-90) or a "scalene obtuse triangle." The two systems are independent.

> [!tip] Equilateral is a special case of isosceles
> An equilateral triangle has *three* pairs of equal sides, so it satisfies the isosceles definition three times over. Every equilateral triangle is isosceles, but not vice versa.

---

## Key Properties

**Angle sum:** $A + B + C = 180°$ in any triangle. This means a triangle can have **at most one** right or obtuse angle.

**Exterior angle theorem:** An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles. This is a direct consequence of the $180°$ sum — see [[Angle Properties (Vocab)]].

**Isosceles base angles:** If two sides are equal, the angles opposite those sides are equal. The converse is also true: if two angles are equal, the sides opposite them are equal.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing "isosceles" and "equilateral."** Equilateral means all three equal; isosceles means (at least) two equal. Every equilateral triangle is isosceles, but a triangle with sides 5, 5, 3 is isosceles and not equilateral.
2. **Assuming the "base" is always at the bottom.** Any side can be the base — what matters is that the height is perpendicular to whichever side you choose.
3. **Forgetting that an equilateral triangle has all angles $60°$**, not $90°$.

## Exam Notes

### OxAQA 9260 / Cambridge 0580

**Syllabus ref:** G1 (9260) / E4.1 (0580). Triangle vocabulary is assumed knowledge — it appears as part of larger geometry questions rather than standalone. "Name the type of triangle" questions are rare but do appear at Core level. More commonly, knowing that a triangle is isosceles unlocks the base-angle property needed to find a missing angle.
